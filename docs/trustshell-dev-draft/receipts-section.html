Receipts, not promises.

A TrustShell action is decided before it runs, recorded while it runs, and sealed into a receipt anyone can check offline — one that is strict about what it refuses to claim. Five steps, no single “valid” badge at the end of any of them.

Step 1Enforce

The owner decides before the tool runs

A policy you own lists what may run and pins each tool to one executable by path and sha256. The decision and its record come before the command starts — a command that is not listed, or whose binary was swapped, never starts.

$ trustshell guard init                                 # write the owner policy; every limit 0
$ trustshell guard allow tool:git --exe "$(command -v git)"   # list it AND pin it by path+sha256
$ trustshell run --tool git -- "$(command -v git)" --version  # decided+recorded, then runs
$ trustshell run --tool git -- ./not-the-pinned-git --version # REFUSED before it starts (exit 5)
With a policy in place, anything unlisted is denied, and no policy at all means nothing runs (exit 2) — never a silent allow. This is where the receipt’s authorization and containment claims come from.
Step 2Observe

What happened is recorded — and so is what no one watched

The run records the plain facts it has: the exit code, the signal, the duration. It does not claim to have watched the work. The card says so in those words, and the record goes into a local, hash-chained log you can check offline.

$ trustshell inspect                     # verify the local tool-call log — opens NO socket
INTACT  .trustshell/session.jsonl      # exit 0 · BROKEN (1) / UNCHAINED (3) / NO_LOG (3)
The run card reads independently observed — nothing: no outside party watched this command. “Exited 0” is recorded as an exit code, never promoted to “succeeded”, “correct”, or “verified”.
Step 3Verify

One receipt, six separate claims

There is no single “valid” badge. verify-receipt recomputes the hash and the signature locally — no network, no engine — and reports each claim on its own, with its own gaps.

$ trustshell verify-receipt r.json --root 0x8e8c…7222 exit 0
Offline verdict — sealer ✓ trusted under the pinned key. No network was used.
  • IntegrityVERIFIEDThe bytes are whole: the hash matches the contents, and a pinned Ed25519 key sealed them.
  • AuthenticityNOT_CHECKEDNo six-claim envelope was supplied, and this cannot be derived from the receipt alone.
  • ObservationNOT_CHECKEDNo outcome (e.g. a payment settling on chain) was observed and attested.
  • CorrectnessNOT_CHECKEDThe content was not verified against its source.
  • AuthorizationNOT_CHECKEDWhether the kernel permitted this action under its policy is not established here.
  • ContainmentNOT_CHECKEDThat the tool ran only under an ALLOW decision is not established here.
NOT_CHECKED is never a pass. Offline verification proves two things by cryptography: the bytes are whole, and a pinned key sealed them. Every other claim is read from an authenticated envelope or stays NOT_CHECKED — so exit is keyed to integrity: 0 VERIFIED, 1 FAILED, 2 NOT_CHECKED.
Step 4Earn

Three levels of evidence, never collapsed

A signature says who sealed a receipt — not that an action was permitted, and not that it earned a reputation. The levels do not blur together, and you can only reach the first one by yourself.

Owner-self-signed you can do this now

Sealed with your install’s own key. Integrity verifies under the key you pin, offline.

Proves authorship and wholeness, nothing more. Not independent verification, and it earns no production RepID.

Issuer-attested the engine’s key

An engine key sealed the receipt and an authenticated six-claim envelope backs it, so a claim beyond Integrity reads VERIFIED.

The engine’s key is not independent of the issuer, so this is not yet independent verification — and still not reputation. Verification and reputation are separate gates.

RepID-eligible the engine decides

Whether a receipt earns portable reputation is the engine’s call, against its own pinned key.

Never an offline verdict, and never self-declared. An owner cannot promote their own receipt here.

Step 5Reuse

Carry what was earned — authority stays home

A bundle is one Ed25519-signed file: your profile, the policy by hash, the notes you choose, and evidence pointers by sha256. Never credentials, keys, or spending authority. What travels is evidence a stranger can check; what does not travel is the power to act.

$ trustshell bundle export carry.sig --notes released   # sign a carry; the key never goes in it
$ trustshell bundle import carry.sig                    # verify its signature, then write it here
On import, authority does not come with it: notes land untrusted and quarantined, and a policy is enforced only if its owner signature verifies with a key you trust and its nonce is higher than the one in force. The same receipt and the same RepID carry across contexts without carrying the right to act.

What a receipt does not prove

  • An owner self-signature proves authorship and wholeness — not that the engine permitted the action, and not that it earned reputation.
  • NOT_CHECKED is never rounded up to a pass. A claim with no evidence stays not-checked; there is no single rollup “valid”.
  • Offline verification is stateless. It does not catch a verbatim replay of one receipt. Replay is prevented by the engine’s receipt-id and nullifier, not by the offline verifier.
  • A pinned root is required to accept a seal. With no key pinned, a whole-but-unaccountable receipt is NOT_CHECKED, never exit 0.
